# Storage and freezing — per recipe

Research input for [comparison.md](comparison.md) rows B16 and D11, and `/wybor/` F3: not a rule
and not yet on the site. Waits on the owner's wife's answer.

## Sources

- **UMN** — University of Minnesota Extension,
  [Storage times for food in the refrigerator and freezer](https://extension.umn.edu/preserving-and-preparing/storage-times-food-refrigerator-and-freezer):
  fridge at 4 °C (40 °F), freezer at −18 °C (0 °F).
- **ILL** — University of Illinois Extension,
  [What Can I Freeze?](https://extension.illinois.edu/sites/default/files/what_can_i_freeze_eng.pdf)
  (March 2025): times "for top quality" at −18 °C; "freezing does not refresh baked goods".
- **NCHFP** — National Center for Home Food Preservation,
  [Storing home-canned jams and jellies](https://nchfp.uga.edu/how/make-jam-jelly/jams-jellies-general-information/storing-home-canned-jams-and-jellies/).

Freezer times in both tables are for quality. FoodSafety.gov and USDA FSIS, the usual primary
sources, refused automated reading (403); UMN and ILL are university extensions repeating USDA's
figures.

## To check before anything goes on a page

- **Dżem:** NCHFP's year assumes jars "processed in a canner for the recommended time". Our
  recipe only turns the hot jars over; the family decides whether the page says so, and NCHFP's
  figure may not hold for it.
- **A category is an approximation.** Schabowy as "cooked meat" is fair; the fritters, the
  cashew cheese and baklava need their own source or stay without a line.
